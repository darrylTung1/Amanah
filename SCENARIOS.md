# Amanah — scenarios, consequences and playthroughs

All characters and events below are fictional. Numeric outcomes are generated by the current engine, not urban forecasts. The eight scenarios, automatic lead selection and parcel interactions are implemented. Narrative props described as proposed remain future visual work.

## The playable loop

1. Open the Singapore map and click Kampong Glam. Enter the council directly, with no introduction or allocation screen.
2. Select a district parcel to open its character, situation and three relevant choices.
3. Consider a policy, read its capacity cost, and hear a prerecorded line when supplied.
4. If a stakeholder holds a veto, use the explicit protection offer. Agreement changes the policy condition; audio playback never decides the result.
5. Commit once. The engine advances to 2036, then 2050, then 2126 after the final decision.
6. See district conditions and hear an exact-path future testimony. Unrecorded paths retain computed written testimony.
7. The receipt reveals who benefited, irreversible changes and their actual first-trigger years.

Three decision years: 2026, 2036, 2050. Three consequence horizons: 2036, 2050, 2126. Starting priorities: 3 affordability, 3 continuity, 2 vitality, 2 equity.

## Scenario selection within the game

Implemented selector for 2036 and 2050: LINEAGE_BROKEN → “The pattern no one can teach”; otherwise EXODUS → “Who gets to belong?”; otherwise HEAT_LOCK or habitability below .40 → “The afternoon nobody stays”; otherwise continuity below .45 → “The empty apprentice’s stool”; otherwise equity below .35 → “Who gets to belong?”; otherwise MONOCULTURE or vitality above .70 with affordability below .50 → “A street everyone visits”; otherwise vitality below .40 → “New keys, old walls”; otherwise revisit “Six weeks to stay” as a new discussion about affordability, without replaying its 2026 lease deadline. Policy-specific conversations can still open when a player selects night economy or adaptive reuse.

This ordering chooses one lead scene. All eight policies remain available through the scene choices and Other policies. Avoid replaying an urgent 2026 deadline in 2050.

## Six weeks to stay

**Place:** Arab Street textile shop · **Speaker:** Mdm Salmah

**When:** Opening round, 2026

> This cloth came through three generations of hands. The new lease asks for more than twice the rent. I can teach someone the trade, but I cannot teach in a shop I cannot afford. What would you protect first?

**Decision:** Keep traders in place, invest in their skills, or build community ownership?

- **Rent covenant:** Buy tenants time, with owner compensation.
- **Trade & apprenticeship grant:** Support apprenticeships; this does not directly reduce rent.
- **Community land trust:** Invest heavily in shared ownership.

**Consequence logic:** Rent protection improves affordability; it does not permanently end rent pressure. A skills grant improves continuity but cannot substitute for affordable premises.

**Board direction:** Focus the Arab Street parcel. Use its existing affordability condition; do not claim an individual eviction unless a future individual-shop model tracks it.

## The empty apprentice’s stool

**Place:** Traditional trade workshop · **Speaker:** Ustaz Rahim

**When:** Later round when continuity < 0.45, unless lineage is already broken

> The tools are still here. The question is who will learn to use them. A photograph can preserve the pattern. It cannot teach the pressure of a hand. What will make learning this trade a possible future?

**Decision:** Support transmission of skills, protect premises, or find a new use?

- **Trade & apprenticeship grant:** Fund teaching and apprenticeships.
- **Rent covenant:** Reduce the pressure that makes staying difficult.
- **Adaptive reuse:** Support a new use with an archive protection clause.

**Consequence logic:** Grants directly support continuity. Reuse improves activity and equity, but its annual affordability penalty can undermine living trades.

**Board direction:** Use the lineage-trades parcel condition. An empty stool is a proposed narrative prop, not an existing animated asset.

## A street everyone visits

**Place:** Bussorah Street · **Speaker:** Jo

**When:** Later round with vitality > 0.70 and affordability < 0.50, or MONOCULTURE

> People come because this street feels alive. Their spending keeps our staff working. But if the people who make the place cannot stay, what exactly are we asking visitors to come and see?

**Decision:** Keep growing visitor activity, redistribute its benefits, or protect tenants?

- **Community visitor levy:** Raise community capacity and equity, with a ten-year sunset.
- **Rent covenant:** Counter affordability pressure.
- **Expand the night economy:** Expand activity, accepting wider social and heat costs.

**Consequence logic:** Higher vitality can intensify rent pressure. If MONOCULTURE is active, that stronger pressure persists even after the mix improves.

**Board direction:** Compare visitor-economy activity with Arab Street occupancy indicators; do not equate a busy street with a healthy district.

## Whose night is it?

**Place:** Food and gathering spaces · **Speaker:** Mdm Salmah

**When:** Any round when considering night-economy expansion

> I understand why the food stalls want longer evenings. They have families to support too. But our working day starts early. If the street stays awake, who makes sure the people upstairs can sleep?

**Decision:** Expand the evening economy, improve daytime access, or invest in cooling?

- **Expand the night economy:** Negotiate a noise curfew before expansion.
- **Pedestrianise & shade:** Improve walking and shade while retaining delivery access.
- **Cooling retrofit:** Improve habitability without directly expanding activity.

**Consequence logic:** The curfew softens the night policy’s habitability and equity penalties and reduces its immediate vitality boost. It does not remove the continuity penalty.

**Board direction:** Night lighting is proposed. Existing board activity and conditions can already show the calculated trade-off.

## The afternoon nobody stays

**Place:** Shade corridor · **Speaker:** Jo

**When:** Later round with habitability < 0.40, or HEAT_LOCK

> Getting people here is only half the work. They need a street they can walk along, somewhere to pause, somewhere to sit. And our deliveries still need a way through. Can we make room for all of that?

**Decision:** Cool existing buildings, redesign the street, or raise money first?

- **Cooling retrofit:** Directly improve habitability.
- **Pedestrianise & shade:** Add shade and walking space with a morning loading window.
- **Community visitor levy:** Raise capacity, without directly repairing heat conditions.

**Consequence logic:** HEAT_LOCK halves positive habitability recovery and adds a vitality penalty. Cooling is more effective before the threshold is crossed.

**Board direction:** Use the existing shade corridor and tree-health changes; heat shimmer and crowd avoidance remain proposed.

## Who gets to belong?

**Place:** Community courtyard · **Speaker:** Faiz

**When:** Later round with equity < 0.35, or EXODUS

> We keep inviting the next generation to take part. Taking part needs space, time, and a reason to believe you can stay. When we say this is a place for everyone, who gets to help make that true?

**Decision:** Change ownership, share visitor income, or invest in learning?

- **Community land trust:** Support affordability and equity at a high capacity cost.
- **Community visitor levy:** Redistribute visitor gains and raise capacity.
- **Trade & apprenticeship grant:** Support skills with a smaller annual equity benefit.

**Consequence logic:** Once EXODUS has triggered, equity cannot rise above 0.50 in this model. Later improvements do not automatically rebuild lost networks.

**Board direction:** Highlight residents and makers, then show the actual equity condition and irreversible flag.

## New keys, old walls

**Place:** Bussorah Street shophouse · **Speaker:** Ustaz Rahim

**When:** Any round when considering adaptive reuse; use when later-round vitality < 0.40

> I am not asking you to leave every room untouched. A building needs a use. I am asking what the new use will remember, and whether the old work has any place inside it.

**Decision:** Change the building’s use, invest in its trade, or cool it for everyday use?

- **Adaptive reuse:** Accept an archive protection clause and its added cost.
- **Trade & apprenticeship grant:** Strengthen living practice.
- **Cooling retrofit:** Improve habitability; this does not directly create economic activity.

**Consequence logic:** Archive protection adds immediate continuity and costs six capacity. It does not cancel the reuse policy’s annual affordability penalty.

**Board direction:** Use the existing continuity and vitality conditions. A detailed before-and-after interior is proposed.

## The pattern no one can teach

**Place:** Traditional trade workshop · **Speaker:** Ustaz Rahim

**When:** Later round with LINEAGE_BROKEN; highest narrative priority

> We can keep the tools. We can keep the records. But a record is not a teacher. Some of what held this trade together has already been lost. What will you protect now, knowing it will not restore everything?

**Decision:** Support remaining knowledge, protect remaining tenants, or sustain public space?

- **Trade & apprenticeship grant:** Support surviving practice, within the recovery limit.
- **Community land trust:** Improve conditions for people who remain.
- **Cooling retrofit:** Protect everyday habitability.

**Consequence logic:** LINEAGE_BROKEN caps annual continuity recovery at 0.003. A grant still has its immediate effect; the flag is never removed.

**Board direction:** Show the flag and its actual trigger year on the receipt. Do not describe a grant as restoring a vanished lineage.

## What the ending can honestly say

The current model is about trade-offs and decaying interventions, not four guaranteed endings labelled “good” or “bad.” Compare 2036 and 2050 as well as 2126. No policy renewals occur after 2050. Several paths therefore converge toward severe decline in the last 76 years. A voice may say that a choice bought time; it must not promise that a shop or lineage survived unless that outcome is represented.

Flag-specific lines must follow state: LINEAGE_BROKEN means annual continuity recovery is constrained; EXODUS caps equity at .50; HEAT_LOCK weakens positive habitability recovery; MONOCULTURE permanently increases the rent-pressure coupling; TRUST_DIVIDEND adds shared capacity and continuity.
