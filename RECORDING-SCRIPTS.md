# Recording scripts — Sociopoly

These are written scripts, not generated audio. Read only the quoted text. Filenames and keys identify the exact scene. Council clips use a natural conversational pace; future testimony is reflective and slightly slower. All characters are fictional.

## tenant_salmah-intro.mp3

Key: `tenant_salmah|intro`

**Direction:** Warm, direct, protective; no caricatured accent.

> My family sells cloth, but what we pass on is how to recognise it, handle it, and work with it. Keeping the shopfront is not enough if the work inside has nowhere to go.

## youth_faiz-intro.mp3

Key: `youth_faiz|intro`

**Direction:** Energetic, thoughtful, impatient with token gestures.

> I want young people to do more than visit this neighbourhood. I want us to make things here, organise things here, and have a say in what comes next.

## custodian_rahim-intro.mp3

Key: `custodian_rahim|intro`

**Direction:** Measured and precise; quietly concerned.

> Heritage is not only what survives in a cabinet. It is what someone still knows how to do, and what someone else has the chance to learn.

## landlord_teo-intro.mp3

Key: `landlord_teo|intro`

**Direction:** Practical, composed; neither villain nor comic relief.

> A promise to keep rents affordable needs a plan behind it. Our obligations do not disappear when we sign. Tell me who carries the cost, and we can discuss the promise.

## operator_jo-intro.mp3

Key: `operator_jo|intro`

**Direction:** Brisk, grounded, personable.

> People need a reason to come here, and businesses need a way to work. I care about the character of this street. I also need our staff and suppliers to get through the week.

## landlord_teo-rent_covenant-hold.mp3

Key: `landlord_teo|rent_covenant|hold`

> You want a limit on rents. I need a compensation plan that makes that limit workable.

## landlord_teo-rent_covenant-compensation_fund.mp3

Key: `landlord_teo|rent_covenant|compensation_fund`

## tenant_salmah-night_economy-hold.mp3

## tenant_salmah-night_economy-noise_curfew.mp3

## keep-the-door-open-2036.mp3

**Voice:** A neighbour, 2036 · **Path:** keep-the-door-open

> I pass Salmah’s shop on Arab Street and look at the batik in the window. Ten years have changed the street around it. There is still room for people to stay, but it takes effort to hold that room open. The old trades are still practised, though their future remains fragile. Visitors and commerce keep the street busy. Some of the gains reach the people who live and work here. The street is still a place people can use. What happens next will depend on who keeps showing up.

## Optional scene openings

These use scene|SCENE_ID keys in the dialogue manifest. The later tenancy variant uses scene|six-weeks|later and must not reuse the 2026 lease-deadline recording.

### scene-six-weeks.mp3

**Speaker:** tenant_salmah · **Scene:** Six weeks to stay

> This cloth came through three generations of hands. The new lease asks for more than twice the rent. I can teach someone the trade, but I cannot teach in a shop I cannot afford. What would you protect first?

### scene-empty-stool.mp3

**Speaker:** custodian_rahim · **Scene:** The empty apprentice’s stool

> The tools are still here. The question is who will learn to use them. A photograph can preserve the pattern. It cannot teach the pressure of a hand. What will make learning this trade a possible future?

### scene-too-busy.mp3

**Speaker:** operator_jo · **Scene:** A street everyone visits

> People come because this street feels alive. Their spending keeps our staff working. But if the people who make the place cannot stay, what exactly are we asking visitors to come and see?

### scene-after-dark.mp3

**Speaker:** tenant_salmah · **Scene:** Whose night is it?

> I understand why the food stalls want longer evenings. They have families to support too. But our working day starts early. If the street stays awake, who makes sure the people upstairs can sleep?

### scene-hot-afternoon.mp3

**Speaker:** operator_jo · **Scene:** The afternoon nobody stays

> Getting people here is only half the work. They need a street they can walk along, somewhere to pause, somewhere to sit. And our deliveries still need a way through. Can we make room for all of that?

### scene-who-belongs.mp3

**Speaker:** youth_faiz · **Scene:** Who gets to belong?

> We keep inviting the next generation to take part. Taking part needs space, time, and a reason to believe you can stay. When we say this is a place for everyone, who gets to help make that true?

### scene-new-keys.mp3

**Speaker:** custodian_rahim · **Scene:** New keys, old walls

> I am not asking you to leave every room untouched. A building needs a use. I am asking what the new use will remember, and whether the old work has any place inside it.

### scene-cannot-relearn.mp3

**Speaker:** custodian_rahim · **Scene:** The pattern no one can teach

> We can keep the tools. We can keep the records. But a record is not a teacher. Some of what held this trade together has already been lost. What will you protect now, knowing it will not restore everything?
